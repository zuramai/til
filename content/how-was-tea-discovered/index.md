The original narratives was that in around 2737 BC, an emperor was drinking a hot water. There were few leaves blown away from the nearby tree into the water, changing its color and taste. The emperor take a sip and pleasantly surprised by the taste.

<br />

But the earliest physical evidence was found in 2 century BCE that tea was consumed by Han dynasty emperors. The tea was identified from genus Camelia. China has the earliest record of tea consumption. They consider tea as a bitter vegetable.

<br />

Then tea use spread to Japan in 6th century. The Japan priest and envoys were asked to go to China to learn their culture. They came back with tea. It became a drink of royal classes when Emperor Saga encouraged the growth of tea in Japan. The seeds were imported from China, and the cultivation started in Japan.
